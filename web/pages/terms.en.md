Earful itself is open source software (AGPL 3.0): anyone can run it, and each instance has its own operator, who answers for it.

By creating an account you accept these terms. If you only answer a survey you need no account, and the section for respondents is the one that concerns you.

## Accounts

- You must be an adult where you live, and able to agree to these terms. If you use Earful for an organisation, you accept them on its behalf and confirm you are allowed to.
- Give an email address that is yours and that you read. It is how you sign in and how we reach you.
- Keep your way of signing in to yourself. What is done from your account is treated as done by you.
- You can close your account at any time from the Account page.

## Your surveys are yours, and so is the responsibility for them

You decide what a survey asks, who is invited to answer it and what is done with the answers. That makes you the controller of your respondents' answers, in the sense data protection law gives the word. We store and process them for you, as the [Trust page](/trust) describes.

In particular, you agree to:

- Tell your respondents who is asking, why, and what will happen to their answers, and have a lawful reason for asking.
- Ask only for what you need. Do not ask for sensitive personal information unless the law allows you to and your respondents know why.
- Invite by email only people you are allowed to write to.
- Keep the promise an anonymous survey makes. Do not try to work out who gave an anonymous answer, by the wording of a question or by any other means.
- Answer your respondents when they ask to see, correct or delete what they told you. We help where only we can.

You keep every right you have in your questions, your texts and your images. You give us only the permission needed to store them and show them to the people you share a survey with.

## Names, logos and images

A survey can carry a name, a logo, images, texts and links of your choosing. Use only names, logos, marks and images that are yours or that you have permission to use.

Do not present a survey as coming from a person, a company or an institution it does not come from. Every survey page names the workspace that is asking, and that line is not yours to remove or contradict.

## What Earful may not be used for

- Deceiving people about who is asking or why, including any kind of phishing.
- Collecting passwords, access codes, payment card or bank details, or copies of identity documents.
- Content that is unlawful, that threatens or harasses, or that is meant to harm the people who see it.
- Sending invitations nobody asked for, in bulk.
- Attacking the service: looking for a way around its limits, loading it on purpose, reaching data that is not yours, or using its AI features for something other than making and reading surveys.

## If you answer a survey

You need no account to answer, and answering is always your choice. The person or organisation that sent you the survey decides what happens to your answers and is the one to ask about them. What this instance does and does not keep about you, your voice included, is on the [Trust page](/trust).

If a survey seems to deceive, to pretend to be someone it is not, or to ask for things no survey should ask for, do not answer it, and tell us. See the contact section below.

## AI features

Earful can draft questions, turn speech into text, translate, and summarise answers. These results come from a language model and can be wrong, incomplete or badly put.

- Read what AI drafts or translates before you publish it. What you publish is yours, however it was written.
- A transcript is shown to the respondent to read and correct before it is sent. It is still only as good as the reading they gave it.
- A summary is a place to start reading your answers, not a replacement for reading them. Do not make a decision about a person on a summary alone.
- AI features have daily limits, and may pause when a limit is reached. The rest of the service keeps working.

## Your data, exports and deletion

What is stored, where, for how long, and which companies are involved is set out on the [Trust page](/trust), and these terms do not repeat it.

You can export everything your workspace holds at any time, in a documented format, and take it to another instance of Earful or anywhere else. What you delete leaves live systems at once and is erased for good within the period the Trust page gives. A published version of a survey, and the answers given to it, cannot be edited afterwards: that is by design, so that what respondents were shown stays on record.

## Reports, and what we may do

Anyone can report a survey to us, from the link at the foot of its pages. When a survey or an account breaks these terms, or we have good reason to think it does, we may close the survey, remove content, or suspend the workspace. Where we can, we tell you first and give you the chance to put it right. Where people are being harmed or deceived, we act first and tell you after.

While your workspace is suspended, its surveys take no answers and show none of their style, and you cannot publish, reopen a survey, send invitations or use AI. You can still sign in, read your results, edit drafts and export your data, and nothing is deleted because of the suspension. When it is lifted, everything is as it was. If we suspend your workspace by mistake, tell us and we will look again. If your account is closed, we give you a reasonable time to export your data, unless the law or the safety of other people prevents it.
